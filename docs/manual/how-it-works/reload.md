# Reload a device

On a device's page, **Actions › Reload…** restarts the device so it boots the startup configuration it holds. It is offered as an operation, never a command: the preview reads the device, shows what else goes down and judges six gates, a verified person confirms with a reason, and the run declares its planned-restart window before anything is sent, then waits for the device and checks it came back as it was. Nothing is configured: a reload sends no configuration, so the startup configuration must already be what the device runs.

A reload cannot be undone.](diagrams/reload.svg)

## The preview

The card reads the device once, in a job, holding nothing: `show running-config`, `show startup-config`, `show version`, `show boot` (a platform without it says so), `dir` of each image named, `dir nvram:` when startup keeps a certificate as a file there, and `show crypto key mypubkey rsa`. Sent: nothing that changes the device. Recorded: nothing. The first six are gates, drawn under **Checks** by name with what each found (pass, fail, at confirm, or needs your reason), followed by the verified-person check; the seventh is drawn above them:

1. **Running against startup.** They must be the same configuration (compared section by section, the self-signed certificate the device regenerates left out, and the `Using … bytes` line `show startup-config` prints above it). Unsaved changes refuse the reload, because a reload boots startup and would lose them; **Persist…** beside it saves them first, previewed. An IOS-XE device keeps the certificates of its own certificate chains as files in nvram:, so startup holds a pointer (`certificate ca 01 nvram:CiscoLicensi#1CA.cer`) where running prints the same certificate inline. That is the same certificate (the same chain, type and serial) when `dir nvram:` lists the file, and the gate says so: "startup keeps edge1's 2 certificates as files in nvram: (both present), running shows them inline". A pointer whose file `dir nvram:` does not list refuses the reload, naming the certificate and the file, and a different certificate is a difference like any other line.
2. **Its golden and its intent.** A device departing from its golden or its committed intent is shown, and your reason is recorded as acknowledging it.
3. **Startup carries Mercury's credential.** Every `username` line the running configuration holds must be in startup, the startup check's own judgement: a reload booting without it would lock Mercury out.
4. **The boot image.** The image `show version` says it booted must exist (`dir`), and the boot variable, where the platform has one, must name a file that exists.
5. **No other operation holds it.** Checked again when the run takes the device.
6. **Its SSH host key survives.** A key pair `write memory` did not save (`Storage Device: not specified`, as a vIOS-L2 switch reads) is made anew at boot: the device comes back with another SSH host key, and Mercury refuses every session to it until a person reviews the new key on its page. A device keeping two saved general-purpose key pairs with neither named for SSH (`ip ssh rsa keypair-name`) may come back serving the other: a C8000v did. Either asks for your reason, recorded as acknowledging it; one saved key, or the named one saved, passes; key pairs not read are said as not known.
7. **What else goes down**, not a gate: shown first and in its own colour, above the checks: every device Mercury loses reach to while this one restarts. It is worked out from records, never a probe: committed intent gives each device's subnets, this host's interfaces give the subnets Mercury is on, and the devices still joined to Mercury without this one are the ones it keeps. A switch's VLAN subnets are taken down with it. What it cannot see is said with it: a path the routing does not use still counts as reach, and a switch carrying a subnet at layer 2 with no address in it is not seen.

The card also says how long the device is out: the run waits up to 2.5 times the platform's measured boot time (an emulated IOS-XE router, about 6.5 minutes), or, on a platform not yet measured, a placeholder it names as one.

## The run

The confirm, **Reload <device> (up to <N> minutes out)**, needs a verified person and a reason in the shape of one. It is bound to the preview's fingerprint (the two configurations, the image, and whether the device departs). The run is a job holding the device, drawn on the one stepper:

1. `read`: the device is read again and judged again. Read: as the preview. Sent: nothing. Recorded: nothing. A device whose fingerprint moved since the preview, or whose gate now fails, is not reloaded, and the result says which.
2. `window`: the planned-restart window is declared, from now until the wait's bound plus five minutes, with you and your reason. Read: the restart record. Sent to the device: nothing. Recorded: the window, with the devices the preview said Mercury loses reach to (What else goes down) as its **expected effects**, and the **far end of every link** the device has in committed intent (the other device on the same IPv4 or global IPv6 network as each of its interfaces): a reload takes every link down, so each neighbour's side of it (its logged adjacency drop, its port's transitions, self-healing's planned-work guard) is the reload's expected effect until the window's end plus the settle margin, never an incident. Read: committed intent too. A window that cannot be recorded, or committed intent that cannot be read (so the far ends cannot be told), stops the run before anything is sent, naming why. Where the network's Grafana is configured, the window also sets a **Grafana silence** on the device's alerts (those naming it by its `device` label, as telemetry, the heartbeat and the syslog rule do, and those naming its management address as `instance`, as the SNMP rule does), as you, with your reason, until the window's end plus a settle margin of 19 minutes: the alerts outlast the device's return (on the first reload measured, of an emulated IOS-XE router, the telemetry alerts were still firing two minutes after it answered). The step says what Grafana did: "silenced until …" with the silence's id, or "Grafana's alerts are NOT silenced: …" naming why (a credential that may not create silences, Grafana not answering). The silence's id is kept on the window, and the window is declared either way.
3. `reload`: `reload` is sent; the device's `[confirm]` is answered, and success is the session dropping. A `Save?` prompt is refused (startup is already what it runs), as is any other prompt, named. Sent: `reload` and its confirmation. Recorded: the drop time.
4. `wait`: the device's management address is asked on SSH's port every 10 seconds until it accepts, within the bound. Read: whether the port answers. Sent: nothing to the device's configuration. A port that answers is not yet a device serving SSH: a C8000v's answered 14 seconds before it logged `%SSH-5-ENABLED`, so the window stays open until verify passes.
5. `verify`: Mercury signs in with the credential it holds and reads the running configuration, which must be what it ran before the reload. A sign-in that fails while the device finishes starting is tried again, pausing 5, 10, 20 and then 30 seconds, for up to 3 minutes after the port answered; the step says on which try it signed in. Read: the running configuration. Sent: nothing. Once it passes, **the window ends** there, not at its declared end, so the device going down again is raised at once; the window's Grafana silence is moved to end the settle margin after now. Recorded: the window's end (`planned_restart_ends.jsonl`). If verify never passes, the reload is recorded unverified and the window runs to its declared end. The session compares the device's SSH host key with the one Mercury holds before it signs in, as every session does ([Re-key](rekey#every-session)): a reload keeps the key the device saved, so the same key passes; a device that comes back with a different key is refused with nothing sent, and verify stops at once, without trying again, naming both keys (the outcome "host key changed"). The reload's window declares no new keys; if the device really did make a new key (it booted from a fresh disk), review it with Re-key on its page.
6. `record`: the run's row (who, why, the window, each step's outcome) is written to the network's repository (`.nsot/reloads.jsonl`) and committed as you, with your reason, then pushed like every commit; History draws it once, as the reload, beside the restart and its window. A row that could be written and not committed says so on the result.

## While it is down

Inside its window the device is **restarting as planned**, not broken: while the reload runs, its page's header, its row on Devices and its node on Topology say "Reloading · step 4 of 6 (wait) · back by ~…", amber and linked to the reload's card, and a device the preview said Mercury loses reach to says "Unreachable as expected · <device> reloading · back by ~…" while it does not answer ([Under a planned operation](devices#planned)). The reload's card lists its **Expected effects**: each signal Needs attention is holding for this reload now (the device not answering, a device out of Mercury's reach, an adjacency with it at one end, its protocols not measured, a neighbour's logged drop, its alerts, a drift run that could not read it), what predicted it, and when it is expected back; a predicted device that already answers says so. Under them, **Unexpected during this reload** lists anything those sources raise about a device the reload did not touch, and **Overdue** anything still wrong about one it did once its time has passed; both stay rows on Needs attention. A source that could not be read is named, never taken as no effect. Needs attention raises no row for it, saying under *What was checked* that it is restarting as planned and who planned it. Its Grafana alerts are held the same way until the window's end plus the settle margin. So is what the reload predictably causes elsewhere, until the same time: a device the preview said Mercury loses reach to ("access1 is out of Mercury's reach as expected while edge1 restarts as planned … expected back by …"), an adjacency with the reloaded device at one end, the reloaded device's protocols that cannot be measured while it is down, a neighbour's logged adjacency drop to it (the neighbour's log names the reloaded device by its router id or address, which Mercury matches to it), and a drift check that could not read either device during the window (the next run reads them again). Each is said under *What was checked*, never a row. A device the preview did not predict is raised as usual, and so is anything still wrong once the settle margin has passed. If it is still not answering when the window ends, Needs attention raises it as not answering, critical, naming the window that ended. The restart itself is recorded when its uptime counter is next read lower than before, however long it was down, and History draws it as **Restarted as planned**.

## When it goes wrong

- **A gate fails:** nothing is offered but **Read it again**, which reads the device afresh; the card names the gate and what it found.
- **It moved since the preview:** nothing is sent; the result says why and offers **Read it again**.
- **It does not come back within the bound:** red, with the time it waited. The way in is the console, with the device's break-glass record; the planned window stays declared until its end, and from then Needs attention raises it.
- **Grafana did not take the silence:** the `window` step says so and why; the reload goes ahead, and Grafana's own notifications for the device are not held. Mercury's Needs attention holds its alerts during the window all the same. The result is never green over it: the card leads with "Grafana's alerts were NOT silenced during this reload: …" naming why, and asks you to tell whoever the alerts page that this was the planned reload; the reload's record keeps the window's silence as failed, with the same words.
- **It comes back and cannot be verified:** amber, "Back, not verified": its port answered and no sign-in succeeded within the 3 minutes, so nobody knows it runs what it ran. Its verify step says when the port answered, how many sign-ins failed over how long, and the last one's error in its first line (never the SSH library's list of possible causes). The window runs to its declared end. Needs attention raises one row for the device until a later record replaces it, and the result offers **Verify again** (below).
- **It comes back running something else:** red, with how many lines went and came (a line the platform rejected at boot is dropped silently). Its golden is not changed by the reload.

## Verify again {#verify-again}

For a reload that ended not verified. Needs attention's row ("<device> came back from its reload and was not verified") opens it on the device's page, and the reload's result offers it too. **Verify again** needs a verified person and runs as a job holding the device, on the stepper:

1. `verify`: Mercury signs in with the credential it holds, trying again as the run's verify does (5, 10, 20 and then 30 seconds, for up to 3 minutes), and compares the running configuration with the device's **committed golden**. The run kept no copy of what the device ran before it; the preview's check that it matched its golden (or your reason that it did not) is what stands in. Read: the running configuration. Sent: nothing. A host key Mercury does not hold stops it at once, naming both keys, as the run's verify does.
2. `record`: its row, kind Verify again, is written beside the reload's in `.nsot/reloads.jsonl` and committed as you. History draws it as **Verify again**.

Its result: **Back, verified** (green; the Needs attention row clears), **Still not verified** (amber; the row stands, now naming this attempt), or **Back, running something else than its golden** (red: the row clears, and the drift check raises the difference, as for any device). It never ends the reload's window: by then the window has run to its end.
- **There is no rollback.** A reload cannot be undone, and the preview says so.

## What a reload does not do

- It sends no configuration, and boots no other file: booting a moment from history is a different operation (revert by reload, the charter's Phase 2).
- It does not check routing adjacencies after the restart: the Neighbours tab reads them at Prometheus's next scrape.
