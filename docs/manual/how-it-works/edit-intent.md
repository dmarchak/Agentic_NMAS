# Edit a device's intent

A device's intent is the committed document that says what it should be: `host_vars/<device>.yml` in its network's repository. A change to a device is made by editing that document and committing it, then deploying; never by configuring the device and reading it back. The editor is on the device page's **Intent** tab: **Edit** turns the read-only card into the editor, in place. Nothing is sent to the device by editing; a deploy does that.

## The steps

1. `open`: the committed document is read from git at HEAD, with the blob it came from. Read: the device's intent as committed. Sent: nothing. Recorded: nothing. The editor opens on exactly that text: what you see is what is committed, byte for byte. A device whose intent is only onboarding's bootstrap is not edited here; Actions › Seed intent… gives it its first full intent.
2. `check`: the document is checked as you type, and rendered. Read: the device's template and its captured golden. Sent: nothing. Recorded: nothing. An error is named with its line and column (YAML, the device it names, an interface key nothing reads, a key in a place intent does not read it, such as a BGP block's `asn` one level up under `routing` instead of under `routing.bgp`, named with where intent does read it, a syslog block that is not whole, an interface holding two keys that undo each other (`shutdown` with `no_shutdown`, `no_ip_address` with an address, `no_switchport` with a `switchport` key: both lines would render and the device would keep the later one), a run's notes as a description, a secret value or a character the device would not accept), and the commit waits until there is none. Beside the document: what your edit changes in the device's configuration (the render of your edit against the render of what is committed, the monitoring profile's lines on both sides), and what a deploy would send after this commit (against the device's golden, merge-only). Each changed line is its own row, under its section's line. When the two are the same (the deploy's lines are exactly what your edit adds, and each line it deletes is one setting the new line replaces), one diff is drawn with "A deploy would send exactly this change". When they differ, both are drawn: the deploy's lines from your edit apart from those not from it, each of these labelled from the device's golden history (held by an earlier golden and gone since: changed on the device; never held: committed and not yet deployed; "already pending" when the history cannot say), and any line your edit deletes that a merge-only deploy keeps is named, with Remove lines (Mode B) as the way to remove it.
3. `acknowledge`: the lines the parser does not model are listed with whether each is acknowledged. Read: nothing more. Sent: nothing. Recorded: nothing until you commit. A template cannot render such a line, so a deploy, and a template's approval, wait until each is acknowledged: a person saying the device keeps it as it is. Ticking lines and writing them into the document sets its `unmodeled_ack` block, which you see in the document and commit with your reason. Lines the device writes for itself (its certificates, licence UDI, self-signed trustpoint, and AutoInstall's DHCP client-id) are never listed: they are the device's own.
4. `commit`: one commit of the document, as you, with your reason as its subject. Read: the intent at HEAD again, under the repository lock. Sent: nothing. Recorded: the commit (`host_vars: <device> <your reason>`), with who you are and `Source: intent-edit`. Nothing is sent to the device: it now departs from its intent. The result draws how, against its golden, apart: the lines a deploy would send (under "What a deploy would send now", with **Plan a deploy…**), and the lines only the device holds, which a merge-only deploy never removes. When a deploy would send nothing (an edit that only deletes), the result is headed by the departure ("How edge1 now departs from its intent", for edge1), draws those lines, and its action is **Remove lines (Mode B)…**, which opens the device page's deploy card at the removal, each line selected with a reason. When it would send lines and the device also holds lines intent no longer has, both are drawn, **Plan a deploy…** the primary action and **Remove lines (Mode B)…** beside the lines it keeps.
5. `moved`: if the device's intent was committed by someone else after you opened it, nothing is written. Read: what you opened and what is committed now. Sent: nothing. Recorded: nothing. The card names both versions and who moved it, with their change and yours. When the two touch different lines, yours is placed on theirs and the editor opens on the result, so you see it and its diffs before you commit; when both change the same lines, the editor opens on theirs and your document stays beside it to copy from.

Whatever you type, the commit writes the document in Mercury's own formatting: the data you typed, every key kept, its comments and layout Mercury's. The check says so before you commit when your text is laid out otherwise, and the result says so after. A change of comments or layout alone therefore commits nothing.

## Normalise the formatting {#normalise}

A document laid out by hand (a list indented under its key, a comment, other quoting) does not round-trip through Mercury's serialiser, and a patch rewrites a document through it: **Change it in words** and **Bulk intent** refuse such a document rather than drop its layout. The Intent tab says so above the document ("This document is hand-formatted") and offers **Normalise the formatting…**, only for such a document.

Normalise opens the editor on Mercury's own formatting of the committed document, at the version committed, and says how many lines changed formatting. Nothing is written. The check then sees an edit that changes formatting only, and draws its proof in place of the diffs, five lines, each a comparison against what is committed now:

1. The parsed document is equal to the committed one.
2. The configuration it renders is byte-identical to the committed render: both masked, compared as text.
3. The device diff is empty: the render against the device's committed golden, as a deploy would compare it. A device with a change committed and not yet deployed is normalised after that deploy.
4. The masked lines were compared by keyed hash, "N of N identical", naming their kinds (the enable secret, a local user's secret, an SNMP community). Masking hides a secret's value, so a render compared masked cannot see a change inside one, and a formatting change that altered a credential could lock the device out. Each masked line of the two true renders is compared by an HMAC under a key Mercury makes at random when it starts and keeps only in memory: no value is shown, no hash is kept or shown, and a line that differs is named by its number and masked text, never its value. The lines compared are exactly the lines the masking hides.
5. The new text round-trips, so a patch can change it.

When all five pass, **Commit the formatting only** commits it as you, with your reason, recorded with the proof's counts (`Formatting-Only:`). Nothing is sent to the device, and a deploy afterwards would send nothing. When any line differs, the commit stays disabled, and every differing line is listed, masked: the render's, the device diff's, and a masked line by its number. The server proves it again when you commit, against what is committed then, and refuses unless every line passes, whatever the page showed. Mercury also checks every network's committed intent for documents that do not round-trip, and Needs attention names each one, with Normalise as its action ([Needs attention](needs-attention)).

## What editing does not do

- It contacts no device and changes no configuration: a deploy does that.
- It never commits for you: a moved intent is shown, and you commit again.
- It never changes a secret's value: the document holds only references.
